.12 Minimum Requirements for Policies and Procedures.

A. The pharmacy shall ensure that a supervising pharmacist shall maintain a policy and procedure manual, reviewed annually, that sets forth in detail the pharmacy’s standard operating procedures with regard to compounding sterile preparations.

B. The pharmacy shall ensure that the policy and procedure manual that sets forth the standard operating procedures with regard to compounding sterile preparations is implemented and adhered to.

C. The policy and procedure manual shall include policies and procedures governing the following:

(1) A risk-management program which includes documentation of outcomes including, but not limited to:

(a) Environmental excursions;

(b) Adverse drug reactions;

(c) Preparation contamination; and

(d) Recalls of compounded sterile preparations prepared by the pharmacy.

(2) Security measures ensuring that the premises where sterile compounded preparations are stored and prepared are secured, to prevent access by unauthorized personnel;

(3) Equipment including, but not limited to:

(a) Procedures for use;

(b) Documentation of appropriate certifications; and

(c) Documentation of appropriate calibration and preventive maintenance if applicable;

(4) Sanitation and cleaning standards and procedures;

(5) Maintenance and availability of reference materials as set forth in Regulation .16 of this chapter;

(6) Information concerning drug:

(a) Preparation;

(b) Storage and handling;

(c) Dispensing;

(d) Labeling;

(e) Beyond-use dating;

(f) Release testing and verification;

(g) Delivery;

(h) Destruction;

(i) Recalls; and

(j) Returns;

(7) Patient record keeping as set forth in Regulation .07 of this chapter;

(8) Facility and equipment control monitoring as set forth in Regulation .07 of this chapter;

(9) Facility certification and viable testing program, as described by US Pharmacopeia 797 Standards;

(10) Handling, dispensing, and documentation of investigational drugs;

(11) A quality assurance program;

(12) Training verification procedures, including:

(a) Gloved thumb and fingertip sampling;

(b) Visual observation for adherence to aseptic technique; and

(c) Media-fill testing with post gloved fingertip and thumb, and surface sampling;

(13) Description of appropriate garb donning and doffing processes;

(14) Conduct guidelines for personnel in the controlled areas;

(15) Personnel responsibilities including:

(a) Pharmacists who are not physically involved in the compounding process;

(b) Compounding staff; and

(c) Cleaning and maintenance staff who have access to the cleanroom;

(16) Patient education, unless in an inpatient setting;

(17) Protocols and procedures to maintain the integrity of the cleanroom suite and the primary engineering controls;

(18) Handling of hazardous drugs as per US Pharmacopeia 800 Standards, if applicable, including:

(a) Utilizing the proper equipment and supplies;

(b) A statement that compounding shall be conducted within a properly certified biological safety cabinet or compounding aseptic containment isolator;

(c) Proper use of personal protective equipment; and

(d) Proper techniques to prevent both contamination of the preparation and chemical exposure of the individual preparing the medication;

(19) Segregation of biological and hazardous material from other compounding activities to prevent cross-contamination, if applicable;

(20) The disposal of infectious materials or materials containing hazardous drugs or waste, as per USP 800 Standards, if applicable;

(21) Written documentation of policy and procedure changes based on data gathered from quality assurance evaluations; and

(22) Assurance of the sterility and stability of compounded sterile preparations including end-product testing and sterilization, if applicable.
